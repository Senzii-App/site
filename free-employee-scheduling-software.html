8 tools honestly compared — limits, hidden costs, and the right time to upgrade to a paid solution that actually scales.

Why "Free" Scheduling Software Usually Isn't

Most "free" employee scheduling tools hit a wall fast. The common pattern: generous free tier for 1-10 employees, then a cliff. Or the free version is so stripped down — no shift swaps, no compliance tracking, no multi-location support — that you end up managing schedules in spreadsheets anyway.

Here's what we evaluated across 8 popular free options in 2026:

  • Employee limit — How many staff before you pay?
  • Core features — Shift creation, swap requests, time-off management
  • Hidden costs — SMS charges, multi-location fees, integration paywalls
  • Compliance — Break rules, overtime alerts, fair-scheduling law support
  • Upgrade price — What you'll actually pay when you outgrow free

The 8 Best Free Employee Scheduling Tools (2026)

1. Homebase — Best Free Plan for Small Restaurants & Retail

Free tier: 1 location, unlimited employees, basic scheduling + time clock.
Limit: No multi-location, no advanced labor reporting, no POS integrations on free.
Upgrade: $20/location/month for Essentials; $48/location for Plus.
Verdict: Solid if you're a single-location cafe or boutique. The moment you add a second location, the math changes fast.

2. Sling — Best Free Plan for Shift Swaps & Communication

Free tier: Unlimited users, scheduling, task management, news feed.
Limit: No time-off tracking, no PTO accruals, no payroll integrations.
Upgrade: $1.70/user/month for Premium; $3.40 for Business.
Verdict: Good internal communication tool. Weak on compliance and labor law tracking. Upgrade pricing is per-user, which is fair.

3. When I Work — Best Free Trial, Not Free Forever

Free tier: 14-day free trial only. No ongoing free plan for businesses.
Limit: N/A — it's a trial.
Upgrade: $2.50/user/month for Essentials; $5.00 for Advanced.
Verdict: Powerful platform, but "free" is misleading. At $5/user, Senzii matches their Advanced tier price with a simpler, all-features-included model.

4. Deputy — Best for Compliance, But No Free Plan

Free tier: 31-day trial. No permanent free tier.
Limit: N/A.
Upgrade: $4.50/user/month for Scheduling; $5.50 for Premium (includes time & attendance).
Verdict: Deputy is excellent for break compliance and fair workweek laws. But there's no free ride — and their Premium tier costs more than Senzii's flat $5/seat.

5. Google Calendar + Sheets — Free Forever, But Manual

Free tier: Completely free.
Limit: Zero automation. No conflict detection. No shift-swap workflow. No labor cost tracking. No compliance alerts.
Upgrade: Google Workspace starts at $6/user/month.
Verdict: Works for 3-5 people who know each other well. At 10+ staff, the manual overhead kills productivity. You're paying in manager hours, not dollars.

6. Connecteam — Best Free Plan for Deskless Teams

Free tier: Up to 10 users, scheduling, task management, basic time clock.
Limit: 10-user hard cap. No GPS tracking, no job-specific skills matching.
Upgrade: $29/month flat for up to 30 users; $49 for up to 100.
Verdict: Great for very small field teams. The 10-user wall is abrupt. Their paid pricing is flat-rate (not per-seat), which can be cheaper than per-seat tools at exactly 25-50 users.

7. Zoho People — Free for 5 Users, Then Per-Seat

Free tier: Up to 5 employees, basic scheduling + leave tracking.
Limit: 5-user hard cap. No shift swapping, no open-shift filling.
Upgrade: $1.50/user/month for Essential; $2.50 for Professional.
Verdict: Cheap upgrade path, but the 5-user cap is tiny. Better suited to micro-businesses than growing teams.

8. 7shifts — Free for Single-Location Restaurants

Free tier: 1 location, up to 30 employees, scheduling + time-off requests.
Limit: No sales-labor integration, no tip pooling, no multi-location.
Upgrade: $29.99/location/month for Entrée; $69.99 for The Works.
Verdict: Restaurant-specific features are strong. Per-location pricing gets expensive if you run 3+ locations.

When "Free" Stops Being Free

Three signals that it's time to move off a free plan:

  1. You're managing schedules in 2+ tools. If you're using Google Calendar + a group chat + a spreadsheet for availability, the "free" tool isn't solving the problem. You're just paying with manager time instead of subscription fees.
  2. Compliance risk is real. Free plans rarely include break-rule enforcement, overtime alerts, or fair-scheduling law compliance. One missed break lawsuit costs more than a decade of scheduling software.
  3. You can't scale. Most free plans have hard employee caps (5, 10, or 30). When you hit the cap, you can't just "make do" — you have to migrate data, retrain staff, and risk downtime.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there truly free employee scheduling software?
Yes — tools like Google Calendar, Sling (unlimited users, limited features), and Homebase (1 location) offer genuinely free tiers. But all have functional limits that become painful at 10+ employees or multi-location operations.
What's the best free scheduling app for restaurants?
7shifts offers the strongest free plan for restaurants (30 employees, 1 location). Homebase is a close second with unlimited employees on 1 location.
Can I use Google Calendar for employee scheduling?
For very small teams (under 5), yes. For anything larger, the lack of conflict detection, shift-swap workflow, and labor cost tracking creates more admin work than it saves.
When should I switch from free to paid scheduling software?
The right time is usually when you hit one of these: multi-location operations, 10+ employees, compliance requirements (break laws, fair scheduling), or the need for automatic shift-filling and skill matching.
